A hallmark of human intelligence is its remarkable data efficiency. A child learns to manipulate new objects after interacting with just a few toys, effortlessly generalizing their skills to new situations. In contrast, modern AI, despite its recent successes in data-rich domains like vision and language, has not yet produced similarly capable agents. This disparity stems from a core limitation: robotics is a fundamentally data-scarce domain, lacking the massive, grounded datasets that fuel today's large-scale models. My research bridges this gap by building systems that perceive, reason, and adapt to the physical world, driving *data efficiency* and *scalable generalization* in robotics. To achieve this, I am pioneering *Structured Physical Intelligence*: methods that ground learning-based models with embedded structures and compositional priors.
