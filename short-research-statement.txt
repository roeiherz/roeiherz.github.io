My research aims to build learning systems that allow agents to continually perceive, reason, and adapt to the physical world, enabling data efficiency and scalable generalization in robotics. While this goal echoes the foundational principles of Computer Vision and Robotics, the internet era shifted the field’s focus away from embodied intelligence. Instead, attention turned to data-rich, large-scale models, which fueled major advances in areas like visual recognition and chatbots. Yet, this progress has not extended to physical intelligence—agents that perceive, learn, and act in the real world—as robotics fundamentally lacks the massive, grounded datasets these modern methods require. Truly capable agents must learn continually and generalize beyond the training distribution to new robots, environments, and tasks, capabilities that end-to-end neural networks still lack. To close this gap, I am pioneering Structured Physical Intelligence methods that ground learning-based models with embedded structures and compositional priors to offer a principled solution to the data scarcity challenges in robotics.
